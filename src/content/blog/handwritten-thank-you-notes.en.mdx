# How to write a thank you note people actually keep

A text says "thanks." A **handwritten thank you note** says "you matter enough that I spent twenty minutes and a stamp on you." That's why the note survives every technology shift — and why etiquette still expects one after weddings, showers, job interviews, and significant gifts.

If your excuse is "I don't know what to write" or "my handwriting is a disaster," both are fixable. Here's the four-part formula, copy-ready examples, and a printing option for the handwriting-phobic.

## The four-part formula (works for every occasion)

1. **Greet the person by name** — "Dear Aunt Rachel," not "Hi";
2. **Name the gift specifically** — "the Le Creuset Dutch oven" beats "your generous gift";
3. **Say what it means or how you'll use it** — this is the part people remember: "Sunday soups just got an upgrade";
4. **Close the loop** — look forward: "Can't wait to have you over to try it. Thank you again — with love, Sam."

Four sentences. If you can fill those four slots, you can write a thank you note.

## Copy-ready examples

| Occasion | The one sentence that does the work |
| --- | --- |
| Wedding gift | "We registered for this dish set three years ago and finally own it — every dinner party this year will have you to thank." |
| Job interview | "Our conversation about how the team handles launches made this the role I want — I'd be thrilled to join." |
| Teacher | "You noticed when he stopped raising his hand, and you fixed it. We will remember that long after this year." |
| Baby shower | "The hand-knitted blanket is already the nap spot of record — she looks like she picked it herself." |
| Host / hostess | "Your table is the reason we didn't want the night to end. Next dinner is at ours." |
| Neighbor / favor | "You were there before we even asked. That's the definition of a good neighbor." |

## "But my handwriting is terrible"

Three honest options, in order of preference:

1. **Slow down 30%.** Most "bad handwriting" is actually "rushed handwriting" — bigger letters, wider spacing, and a 3-second pause between words read as neat;
2. **Use lined or three-line paper as rails.** [Printable ruled paper](/printable-paper) keeps baselines honest; slide a printed sheet under nicer stationery as a guide;
3. **Render it in a real handwriting font.** Paste your words into the [handwriting tool](/), pick **Caveat** (casual) or **Cedarville Cursive** (elegant), and print — the words are still yours, and it lands far warmer than a typed card. For **job interviews**, though, write it yourself: the note's point is the effort.

## Timing rules of thumb

- **Interviews**: within 24 hours, one short paragraph, one specific moment from the conversation;
- **Weddings**: within 3 months (gift received, not gift given, starts the clock);
- **Everything else**: within a week is ideal; a late handwritten note still beats a prompt text.

## People also ask

- **Do you still send handwritten thank you notes after an interview?** Yes — a majority of hiring managers consider them influential, and almost nobody is penalized for sending one.
- **How long should a thank you note be?** Four sentences fits a standard card; longer only if you have a story to tell.
- **What do you write when the gift was cash?** Thank the thought and name the plan: "Your generous gift is going straight into the new-bike fund."
- **Is it okay to print a thank you card in a handwriting font?** For casual thanks, yes — the words carry the warmth. For interviews and formal occasions, handwrite it.
