# Transcribe recordings into handwritten notes, free and local

A two-hour meeting recording, a lecture, a voice memo full of ideas — turning them into a shareable handwritten note used to mean four steps: transcription app, copy, layout, export.

We compressed it into one: **upload an audio file, get a handwritten page**. Free, with the audio never leaving your device. Here's how it works, our real test results, and the honest limitations.

## The three routes, compared

| Route | Cost | Privacy | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| In-browser Whisper (ours) | 0 | Audio stays on device | Daily notes, privacy-sensitive content |
| Cloud ASR (iFlytek, Doubao, Groq…) | Per minute or limited free tier | Audio uploaded | Very long files, speed-critical |
| Phone dictation + copy-paste | 0 | Depends | One-off short snippets |

The local route's only cost: a one-time model download, and transcription speed depends on your hardware.

## How we implemented it (the technical bits)

- Files are decoded via the browser's `decodeAudioData` and resampled to 16kHz mono;
- The Whisper model (~80MB fast / ~250MB high-quality, q8 quantized) is **self-hosted on our own domain**, chunked into 30-second windows;
- Desktop Chrome with WebGPU gets GPU acceleration; everything else falls back to WASM (CPU);
- The transcript lands in the same editor — fonts, paper, export all behave identically to typing.

We self-host the model instead of calling the HuggingFace CDN to avoid cross-origin blocks and keep downloads working in China.

## Real test: a TTS sample

Environment: macOS + Chrome, no discrete GPU (WASM path), fast model.

- Input: synthesized Chinese speech saying "你好,这是一段测试语音,用来验证音频转写功能。" (~5s)
- Output: "你好,這是一段測試語音,用來驗證音頻轉寫功能。"

Content fully correct, but rendered in traditional characters — a known tendency of base-tier models; switch to the high-quality tier or fix by hand. First model load took about a minute; afterwards the browser caches it.

## Three steps

1. On the [tool page](/), click "Import audio file" and pick mp3 / wav / m4a;
2. Wait for the progress hint (includes first-time model download);
3. The transcript lands in the editor — fix typos, pick a style, export.

## Limitations, stated plainly

- Base-tier accuracy degrades with heavy accents and noise; use the high-quality tier and proofread for anything important;
- 30+ minute files take noticeably long — split them;
- Safari's decoding of some audio codecs is incomplete; desktop Chrome / Edge recommended.


## Tested, with honest limitations

Every step was tested on **desktop Chrome (macOS, October 2026)**; screenshots are from real runs. Speech recognition is provided by the browser vendor and quality varies — proofread important text. We collect no text or audio — see the [Privacy Policy](/privacy). Technical reference: [MDN · Web Speech API](https://developer.mozilla.org/docs/Web/API/Web_Speech_API).
