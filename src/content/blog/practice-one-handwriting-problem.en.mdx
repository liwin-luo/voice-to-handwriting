# How to fix one handwriting problem

Open the [sentence practice sheet](/handwriting-sentence-practice), type one sentence you actually have to write this week, and choose one thing: size, slant, spacing, or print. The page gives you a solid line, a dotted line, and three blank lines. When those three are full, stop.

I used to "practice" by filling a page and changing three things at once. The last lines were worse than the first, because the hand had clamped down and I was rehearsing the clamp. One problem, three lines, pen down.

## Pick the one a reader would notice first

Write the sentence once on scrap paper. Circle the row below that fails first. Size and the baseline are the same drill on this sheet: both are about whether the letters hold one height and sit on the line. I used to print two almost identical pages for those. One is enough.

| What you see | Choose | What the sheet adds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Letters change height, or the line sags and climbs | Size | A baseline, and a midline at half the letter height. No top line, no descender line. |
| Letters in the same word lean different ways | Slant | Parallel lines, 15° off vertical, leaning right. |
| Words crash together | Spacing | A short tick on the baseline at each space. No box around the letters. |
| The letter shapes themselves are the jumble, and you want a plain print model | Print | A baseline only. The model locks to Patrick Hand. |

The model starts as Patrick Hand. Turn on the script model and it switches to Sacramento. Print turns that switch off and keeps it off. Both are open fonts already on this site. Neither is your handwriting.

A sentence with no spaces can still use spacing. You get the sentence and the baseline, and no ticks. Word spacing needs a space. Chinese, Japanese, and Korean sentences often have none. Add one where the gap should be, or pick a different problem for that sentence.

If you write with your left hand, the switch moves the red margin to the right so the hand is less likely to smear ink that is still wet. The slant lines flip to lean left only when slant is the thing you chose. It is a way to keep the heel of the hand off the line. It is not the only correct left-hand posture.

## What the five lines are for

This is the whole session. There is no timer.

1. Read the solid line. Do not trace it yet. Check that the one thing you chose is visible: the midline for size, the slant lines, the ticks, or just the baseline.
2. Trace the dotted line once, watching only that thing.
3. Write the sentence on the first blank line.
4. Write it again.
5. Write it a third time. The footer on the page says to stop. That line is instructions, not another practice row.

A sentence has to fit on one line. Past a paragraph and only the first sentence is printed; the page says so next to the box. Past something longer than one line and the page does not draw the paper. It will not shrink the letters, wrap them, or start a second page. Shorten the sentence. "Please read this note." fits. A paragraph does not.

The line height is large, regular, or compact — the same three used on the cursive worksheets, not a preschool grade label. Start on regular. Move to compact when the regular lines already match each other.

## The next time you open the page

Download the PDF or the PNG. That is the only moment this browser stores the sentence, the option, the left-hand switch, the script switch, the line height, and the time. It does not store a picture of what you wrote. Come back and the note above the controls offers to write it again. That sheet is three blank lines and the same guides. You are not comparing a before and an after. The site still cannot see the ink on the paper. Clear the site data and the note goes away.

## The habit that wastes the sheet

Changing the option between the three lines. Size, then slant, then spacing is three unfinished problems. Stay on the one you picked until those three lines agree with each other. Another day, pick the next one.

Also a waste: a weekend of pages. Quantity at a tired grip rehearses the tired grip. Fifteen minutes is already more than this sheet asks. Three lines is the dose. The longer version, one lever per week, is the [four-week plan](/blog/how-to-improve-handwriting-adults). This page is the day you only have one sentence.

## What this does not do

Three matching lines is the win for that day. A steadier version of that one problem shows up over a few weeks of these short sheets. "Beautiful" is a longer road, and this page will not grade it. If the hand hurts, stop. This sheet is not a way to diagnose why. Nothing you type is uploaded.
