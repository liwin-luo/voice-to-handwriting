# Sight word tracing worksheets you can print before homework

The folder comes home on Monday with eight words highlighted, and a line at the bottom that says "please practice." The words are short. The argument about who holds the pencil is not.

A sight word tracing worksheet puts the word on the page already: once in dark ink, then again as a dashed outline, on lines a small hand can hit. A five-year-old is not asked to remember "said" and stay on the line at the same time. One job, then stop.

## Match the list to the age

A sight word is a short word children are asked to recognize on sight — *the, and, said* — because sounding it out on every page slows reading down. Tracing is the handwriting half of that job. It is not a reading program, and it is not a race.

| Age | What goes on the sheet | Row size | Stop after |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **3–4** | Their own name, not a word list. A name gets practiced because it is theirs. | **Pre-K**, the tallest band | One name, then play |
| **5–6** (kindergarten) | 4–6 words from this week's list, or the starter eight below if nothing came home | **Kindergarten**. If letters still spill out of the band, switch to **Pre-K** | About 5 minutes, or two calm words |
| **7+** (1st grade and up) | This week's spelling words, one word per line | Slide the row height to about 80. Use **Grade 2** only when the word already fits | About 8 minutes, then two words copied on blank lines |

The same sheet does not fit all three rows. A three-year-old on an eight-word list is a setup for a fight. A second-grader still on giant letters is practicing the wrong size.

The three lines on the page — top, dashed middle, baseline — are drawn out in [kindergarten handwriting paper](/blog/kindergarten-handwriting-paper).

## Eight words for tomorrow morning

If the folder was empty, start with these. They are ordinary early-reader words, not a full school list:

> the
> and
> see
> look
> come
> play
> you
> my

Print them with these settings:

1. Open the [name tracing generator](/name-tracing). The box is labeled "Names." It takes any word, one per line.
2. Paste **four to six** of them, not all eight. Leave **Dark example row** on. The page writes the list once in dark ink, then repeats it as dashed outlines until the sheet is full. Eight words at the Kindergarten band uses almost the whole page for the dark pass, and the tracing rows only start at the bottom.
3. Font: **Patrick Hand**. Plain print, close to the letters a kindergarten teacher writes on the board.
4. Tap **Kindergarten** for the row height. If the letters still crash into each other, tap **Pre-K**.
5. Paper size: Letter in the US. Download the PDF. No account.

Five minutes at the kitchen table, a regular pencil on the dashed outline, then stop while the mood is still good.

The practice rows are a dashed outline of the whole word, not numbered stroke arrows. Most children can follow the shape. If your child needs "start here, arrow 1, arrow 2," this sheet will not give it — use the stroke card the school already sent, and keep this page for the words.

For a child who is still learning to write their own name, skip the list and make a [name tracing sheet](/blog/name-tracing-generator) instead. The word list can wait.

## When the list is spelling words

Friday spelling uses the same page with different words. Type this week's list, one word per line, and keep it to about a dozen so the rows stay big enough to write in.

A longer packet — a month of words, or one sheet per child in the class — belongs in the [handwriting workbook generator](/handwriting-workbook-generator). Paste the list, up to 60 words. Each word gets one dark example row and two dashed rows, five words to a page. Font **Patrick Hand**, example row on. Print it and pull two pages for this week. The rest stays in the folder.

Words they already trace cleanly move to blank [three-line paper](/printable-paper). They copy two words on empty lines. Tracing teaches the shape. The copy is what shows up on the spelling test.

## If they push the pencil away

Do not add a chart in the moment. Cut the job:

- They pick **two** words. Those two are the whole assignment.
- You point at the dark row, they trace the dashed row once. Done is done.
- A white-knuckle grip, or letters that shrink to specks, means the band is too small or the session ran long. Taller rows, shorter time. The longer version of that adjustment is in [handwriting practice for struggling writers](/blog/handwriting-practice-struggling-writers).

A refused pencil is information. The page usually asked for two skills at once.

## People also ask

- **What is a sight word tracing worksheet?** A page with a few common words printed as a dashed outline on handwriting lines, so a child traces the word instead of starting from a blank line.
- **Are sight word tracing worksheets useful in kindergarten?** For ages 5–6, yes: a tall row (the Kindergarten or Pre-K setting) and a short list. Four to six words, about five minutes.
- **Should a preschooler trace sight words?** Not yet. At 3–4, practice the child's own name on the tallest lines. Word lists can wait until kindergarten.
- **Can the same worksheet be used for spelling words?** Yes. Swap in this week's spelling list, one word per line. Once a word is easy to trace, have them copy it on blank lines.
