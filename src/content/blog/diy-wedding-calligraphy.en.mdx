# DIY wedding calligraphy: place cards & signs without hiring a calligrapher

Professional calligraphy runs **$2–5 per place card** — on a 120-guest wedding, that's a line item. The good news: for most paper goods, guests can't tell DIY from pro at three feet away, and the two that *do* reveal amateur work have workarounds.

Here's how to get calligraphy-style place cards, menus, signs and vow keepsakes from your own printer, free.

## Where DIY script works — and where it doesn't

| Paper good | DIY verdict | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Welcome sign | ✅ Great | Big format forgives everything |
| Menus, bar signs, favor tags | ✅ Great | Read at a glance, printed by the dozen |
| Place cards / escort cards | ✅ Good | See the tracing trick below |
| **Envelope addressing** | ⚠️ Special case | Guests see the mail *before* the wedding; hand-address these |
| Vow / keepsake prints | ✅ Great | You frame it, so your standard is the only one |

## Step 1: set your words in a script font

Open the [handwriting tool](/) and paste your guest list or text. Two fonts cover the wedding look:

- **Dancing Script** — looped and formal, the safe choice for headings, names, and signs;
- **Cedarville Cursive** — lighter, more personal, lovely for vows and quotes;
- Body details (menu items, dates) in **Caveat** keep things airy.

Export as **PDF** — it stays vector-crisp at any print size, unlike screenshots.

## Step 2: print on the right paper

- Place cards: print on white or cream **cardstock (65–80 lb)** and trim;
- Signs: most office/print shops print PDFs on poster stock same-day for a few dollars;
- Menus: standard paper is fine — the font does the work.

One line-color tweak goes a long way: **gray or navy ink reads more elegant than pure black** on cream paper.

## Step 3 (place cards): the window-tracing trick

The honest gap between DIY and pro is **consistent slant across 100 names**. Close it like calligraphers fake it:

1. Print all names from the PDF as a **spacing guide** — same font size, one per line;
2. Tape the guide sheet to a bright window (or a light box if you own one);
3. Lay blank place cards over it, one by one, and **trace the names with a fine-tip pen** — Micron 03 or a gel pen.

Ten minutes per dozen, and every name leans the same way. Since the guide is just printed paper you already have, the only "cost" is tracing time.

## The vow keepsake

Type your vows, set them in Cedarville Cursive on blank paper with generous margins, print on cardstock, and frame. Couples commonly print **his-and-hers pages** for a paired frame — and doing it yourself means the wording can be edited the night before the anniversary print, for free, forever.

## Tested, with honest limitations

- These are **real handwriting fonts, not hand-lettering art** — expect a clean calligraphy style, not flourished copperplate with swashes;
- The editor renders at 2× density, so cardstock prints stay crisp; for anything larger than letter size, use the PDF at a print shop rather than scaling a PNG;
- Free, no signup, and nothing you type leaves your browser.

## People also ask

- **What font is closest to wedding calligraphy?** Dancing Script is the closest free looped style; pair it with Cedarville Cursive for body text.
- **Is it tacky to print place cards instead of hand-lettering them?** No — printed calligraphy-style place cards are standard at weddings; guests notice legibility, not provenance.
- **How do you fake calligraphy at home?** Print the words as a guide and trace them onto the final card over a window — same slant, same spacing, hand-done ink.
- **Can I address envelopes with a handwriting font?** You can, but the invitation suite is where hand-addressing is most noticed — print a guide sheet and trace those, too.
