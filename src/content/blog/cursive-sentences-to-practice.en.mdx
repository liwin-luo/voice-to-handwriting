# Cursive sentences to practice: printable pages for the sentence jump

Last week the letters went fine. This week the assignment says "copy each sentence in cursive," and the pencil stops halfway through the second word. The letters your child could write on Tuesday will not hold hands on Thursday.

That is the normal order of things. A word list asks for one join at a time. A sentence asks for three things at once: holding the baseline across a whole line, leaving a real space between words, and getting through joins that look wrong but are not. A cursive sentence worksheet puts the sentence on the page so the three skills have somewhere to live together — one dark model, then room to try it.

## The joins that look broken — say it before they panic

Four letters sabotage more cursive sentences than any others: **o, b, v, w**. In most cursive hands they finish high, so the line into the next letter looks like a gap. Kids read that gap as "I did it wrong" and start erasing a correct join.

Show them once, slowly: write *go*, *be*, *very*, *we*. Point at the tall finish of the o and the start of the next letter. "This one jumps from up high. That is what it is supposed to do." After that, the gap stops being a crisis and the sentence survives to the end of the line.

## Match the page to the age

| **3–4** | No sentences. Strokes and their own name. | The tallest band on a [name tracing sheet](/blog/name-tracing-generator) | Their name, then play |
| **5–6** (kindergarten) | Words in print, not cursive sentences. If they beg for "real cursive," one traced word a day, just to see it. | **Kindergarten** | About 5 minutes |
| **7–8** (grade 2–3) | One sentence, 3–5 words, once in dark ink and twice as pale rows to trace | **Grade 2** band or taller | One sentence done well |
| **9+** | A model on top and empty lines under it, copying without tracing | Slide the row height down a notch | 8–10 minutes |

Most US schools that still teach cursive start in grade 2 or 3, so a kindergartner asking for cursive is curious, not behind. Let them trace a single word and stop while it still feels like a treat. ## Ten sentences for tomorrow morning

Start with sentences built on the troublemakers. Set A is short and heavy on o, b, v, and w joins on purpose:

> We can go.
> The dog ran.
> Bob saw a van.
> Eve gave a wave.
> Wade wove a web.
> The ox is big.
> I like jam.
> The fox went home.

Set B gives capitals their own workout — capital letters in cursive are a separate lesson, so practice them in sentences, not alone:

> Monday came fast.
> We vote on Wednesday.
> Ava lives on Vine Street.
> Ben visited in October.

Set C, once Set A traces clean — longer lines mean more chances to drift off the baseline:

> The wagon went over the bridge.
> We watched the waves from the window.
> Never give up before breakfast.

## Make the pages: two recipes

### Trace the sentence (grade 2, the first weeks)

1. Open the [name tracing generator](/name-tracing). The box is labeled "Names." It takes any text, one sentence per line.
2. Paste three or four sentences from Set A, one per line. Keep them short — a long sentence in a tall band crowds itself.
3. Font: **Cedarville Cursive**. It is a real connected cursive hand, open source. It is not the font in the school workbook — the joins differ a little, which is fine for practice rows.
4. Row height: **Grade 2**. If a sentence still crowds, shorten the sentence. Do not shrink the writing.
5. Keep the dark example row on. Paper size Letter in the US. Download the PDF. No account.

Each printed line reads: the sentence once in dark ink, then pale copies to trace over. The tracing teaches the whole sweep of the sentence — start to finish, spaces included — without asking for memory at the same time.

### Copy the sentence (once tracing comes out clean)

1. Open the [cursive writing generator](/cursive). Type the sentence — or two, once Set A feels easy.
2. Font **Cedarville Cursive**, or **Dancing Script** if your child prefers a looser look.
3. Export the PDF. That sheet is the model.
4. Print blank lined paper from [printable handwriting paper](/printable-paper) and copy underneath, one line per sentence. Many families staple the model sheet on top of the week's lined pages so the notebook keeps itself.

Copying is a different job from tracing: now the model is on top and memory does the work. Expect the first copied line to be wider and wobblier than the traced one. That gap closes with days, not lectures.

### The zero-prep option

The [daily cursive practice page](/daily-cursive-handwriting-practice) puts a fresh sentence at the bottom of one printable sheet, under warm-ups and letters. No building, no pasting — print and go on the days you have nothing left.

Before a sentence page, two minutes of word-level rows from the [cursive worksheets generator](/cursive-worksheets) warms the joins up. Sentences after words, never cold.

## If the pencil stalls mid-sentence

Cut the job before you coach the grip:

- One sentence is the whole assignment. Not the row, the sentence.
- Write the sentence yourself first, slowly, saying the joins out loud. Watching a real hand beats staring at print.
- Letters shrinking as the line goes on means the row is too tall or the session ran long. Shorter time, and let the last line be the end.
- Baseline drifting downhill: have them run a finger along the printed line before starting, and start each line at the left margin like it is a new sentence. Because it is.

A stalled pencil in week two is information, not defiance. The gentler version of this adjustment — for kids who fight any handwriting — is in [handwriting practice for struggling writers](/blog/handwriting-practice-struggling-writers).

## What these pages will not do

The trace rows are a pale print of the whole sentence, not a dashed outline with numbered stroke arrows. The font is an open-source cursive hand, not your district's workbook hand, so a few joins will look slightly different from the poster in the classroom. Practice still transfers; grading should not be done against the font. And the model does not pretend to be a teacher — it just shows the sentence, connected, one more time.

## People also ask

- **What sentences should a beginner practice in cursive?** Three-to-five-word sentences built from letters they already know, with the high-exit letters (o, b, v, w) mixed in on purpose. Capitals come as full sentences — "We vote on Wednesday" — not as a separate drill.
- **What grade do students start writing cursive sentences?** In most US schools that teach cursive, single letters and joins arrive in grade 2, full sentences in grade 2–3. States differ; some mandate it, some leave it to the district.
- **How do I make cursive sentence worksheets for free?** Paste one sentence per line into a tracing generator that accepts any text, pick a connected cursive font, set the row height to Grade 2 or taller, and download the PDF. The pages above take about a minute.
- **Why do some cursive letters not connect to the next letter?** Letters like o, b, v, and w finish high in most cursive hands, so the join leaves from the top rather than the baseline. The gap is the correct letterform, not a mistake.
