# Pen pal letters for kids: the friendly letter format + printables

Pen pals are quietly having a comeback — classrooms pair up across states, grandparents sign up as "class grandfriends," and scout troops trade letters with troops abroad. For kids, it's the rare writing task with a **real audience and a real mailbox payoff**.

Here's the friendly letter format US schools teach, prompt ideas that unstick writer's block, and the printable paper that keeps 2nd-grade handwriting inside the lines.

## The five parts of a friendly letter

This is the structure teachers grade against (a classroom staple around 1st–3rd grade):

| Part | What it is | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Heading** | Date (and sometimes address), top right | October 12, 2026 |
| **Greeting** | Salutation, followed by a comma | Dear Mateo, |
| **Body** | The news, 1–3 short paragraphs | I lost my first tooth! … |
| **Closing** | Sign-off, lined up with the heading | Your friend, |
| **Signature** | First name only for kids | Lily |

The two kids always forget: the **date** and the **comma after the greeting**. Make a game of spotting them in each other's letters.

## Prompt bank: questions that get answers

The trick to a letter that gets a reply is ending with **questions the other person wants to answer**:

- "What's the weather like where you are? It snowed here — in October!"
- "What's your school lunch like? Ours has pizza Friday."
- "What's something weird that happened to you this week?"
- "If you could have any pet, real or imaginary, what would it be?"
- "What's the best book your teacher has read to you?"

One piece of news + two questions = a letter that writes itself in ten minutes.

## People also ask

- **What are the five parts of a friendly letter?** Heading, greeting, body, closing, and signature — with a comma after the greeting and the closing lined up with the date.
- **What is a good first letter to a pen pal?** Introduce yourself with three facts (name, age, something you love), describe one day-in-the-life detail, and end with two questions.
- **How often should pen pals write?** Once a month is sustainable for classrooms; fortnightly works for enthusiastic pairs. Faster than that, replies become homework.
- **Where can kids find safe pen pals?** Through school or troop programs, relatives, and vetted services — and keep personal details like addresses out of the letters themselves.
