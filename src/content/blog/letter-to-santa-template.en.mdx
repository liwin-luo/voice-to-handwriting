# Free printable letter to Santa template (that kids can actually write on)

Every December, parents search for a letter to Santa template — and most of what they find is either a decorated PDF you can't type into, or a blank page their 5-year-old can't yet write on.

Here's a workflow that solves both: **speak or type the child's words, print them in a friendly handwriting style, and let your child trace over them and sign**. Free, no signup, done in about ten minutes.

## What goes in a letter to Santa

Keep it to five moves — this is the template structure:

1. **Greeting**: "Dear Santa," (or "Dear Santa and the elves");
2. **A kindness report**: one true thing the child did this year — shared with little brother, learned to swim. This is the part Santa "loves reading most";
3. **The wish list**: 2–3 things, stated with conviction;
4. **A question for Santa**: "How do the reindeer fly?" — invites the reply letter;
5. **Sign-off**: "Love, ___" and a signature (this is where the tracing magic comes in).

Sample text to copy into the tool:

> Dear Santa,
> My name is Mia and I am five. This year I shared my toys with my little brother and I learned to ride a bike without training wheels! For Christmas, may I please have a unicorn stuffed animal and art supplies? How do the reindeer fly? I will leave out cookies for you and carrots for the reindeer.
> Love, Mia

## Turn it into a printable in three steps

1. Open the [handwriting tool](/), pick a playful font — **Patrick Hand or Indie Flower** look like a kid's own print writing;
2. Paste the letter, choose blank or ruled paper, and download the **PDF**;
3. Print on Letter paper — done. Optionally age it: tea-stain the edges, roll it, and tie with twine for the "arrived from the North Pole" look.

## The signature: trace-your-name bonus

If your child can't sign their own name yet, generate a [name tracing sheet](/name-tracing) with **"Love, Mia"** on the three-line guides. They trace it in pencil, you leave a copy "for Santa," and the kept original gets the traced signature — a December 1st-to-25th fine-motor exercise disguised as Christmas magic.

## Mailing it (for real)

- **USPS Operation Santa**: letters go to **123 Elf Road, North Pole, 88888** — through the program, some letters are "adopted" and answered by volunteers. Never put a full home address inside the letter; use the program's guidelines;
- **Canada Post**: Santa's own postal code, **H0H 0H0**, and envelopes get a reply in the same language;
- Either way: photograph or scan the letter before it goes. The PDF you printed is the backup that goes in the keepsake box.

## People also ask

- **What address do I mail a letter to Santa?** In the US, USPS Operation Santa uses 123 Elf Road, North Pole, 88888; in Canada, Canada Post answers letters to Santa, North Pole, H0H 0H0.
- **What age should kids write to Santa?** Any age — toddlers dictate while a parent writes (or speaks it into this tool), and by 6–7 many kids write the final copy themselves.
- **How do I get a reply letter from Santa?** Enroll through USPS Operation Santa or Canada Post's program before early December — both publish yearly deadlines.
