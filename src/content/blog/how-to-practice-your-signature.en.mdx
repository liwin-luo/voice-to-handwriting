# How to practice your signature for seven days

Here is how to practice your signature after you have already picked one form. Designing the letters is the other page: [write your name in cursive, then decide the skeleton](/blog/cursive-name-signature). This page is the week you stop shopping for a prettier font.

I printed "Jordan Lee" on the [signature practice sheet](/signature-practice) in Great Vibes. The full name is one long gesture. "J. Lee" sits on a receipt line. The sheet puts the four scripts on single lines so you can see them, then gives the script you selected a solid line, a dotted line, and blank lines on one baseline. That is the whole drill.

## Pick the short form before the pretty one

| Form | Use it when | Jordan Lee |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Full name | You still want every letter readable | Jordan Lee |
| First name | You need volume, and the first name is what you will write twenty times | Jordan |
| Initial + last name | It has to fit a card terminal | J. Lee |

If you cannot write the full name twice in about four seconds, practice the short form. A long name that looks good on a worksheet falls apart at the counter. The sheet splits on spaces only. "de Luca" will not be parsed for you. Type the form you actually want.

Under U.S. commercial law, ["signed" includes any symbol you adopt with the intent to accept a writing](https://www.law.cornell.edu/ucc/1/1-201). A shortened mark can be a signature. That definition is the Uniform Commercial Code, not a rule for deeds, passports, or your bank's signature card. Those offices can still ask for a name they can read.

## Seven days, fifteen minutes

Stay on one script. Great Vibes is the default on the sheet. Alex Brush, Allura, and Mr Dafoe are the other three, all SIL Open Font License models, [the license text is here](https://scripts.sil.org/OFL). They are not your handwriting.

| Day | On the sheet |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Trace the solid line five times. Do not add a tail yet. |
| 2 | Trace the dotted line, then fill two blank lines while looking at the model. |
| 3 | Cover the model. Write the form ten times. Circle the one whose length and slant match. |
| 4 | The same ten, at receipt speed. Cross out any that grew an extra loop. |
| 5 | Write two lines of ordinary notes first, then sign ten times. A tired hand is the test. |
| 6 | Sign on a blank index card, no printed line. If the shape collapses, go back to day 3. |
| 7 | Three signatures side by side. Same slant, same length, same ending. If two of the three match, that is the one. Use it on paper for a month. |

Two weeks of this and the mark starts to feel automatic. "Looks like calligraphy" takes longer, and a signature does not need it. Fifteen minutes is the dose. A Sunday marathon does not stick.

## The habit that wastes the week

Switching scripts every night. Each font teaches a different skeleton, so your hand never keeps one. The comparison lines are for day 1. After that, turn the comparison off and print only the script you chose.

Also a waste: tracing your full legal name in a school cursive and calling that a signature. School cursive is for someone else to read. A signature may drop letters. It may not be a different shape every time you are in a hurry.

## What the sheet will not do

- It will not sign a PDF. You are learning a mark your hand can repeat. A typed script dropped onto a contract is still a font.
- There are no stroke-by-stroke arrows. Trace the whole form as one gesture.
- Nothing you type is uploaded. The PDF is made in the browser.

If day 7 still gives you three different shapes, you picked a form that is too long, or you changed fonts. Shorten it and repeat days 3 through 5. Do not start a new alphabet.
