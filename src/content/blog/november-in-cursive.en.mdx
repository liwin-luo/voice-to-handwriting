# November in cursive: how to write the month (+ all 12 months)

Every November, cursive searches spike on this one word — planners get titled, calendars get lettered, and bullet-journal people want the month header to look right. Good instinct: **a month name is one of the best cursive drills there is**, because it's a real word you'll write thirty times this month, and "November" quietly packs three different lessons into eight letters.

Here's how to write it, what trips people up, and the same drill for every other month — with a free printable at the end. Everything below was rendered and tested in the [cursive writing generator](/cursive) (desktop Chrome, macOS, November 2026).

## The capital rule first: N stands alone

The single most common mistake in "November" has nothing to do with the lowercase. **Capital letters don't connect to the next letter.** In flowing cursive it's tempting to drag the N's last stroke into the *o* — don't. You write the [capital N](/cursive/letter/capital-n) (two round arches over a looping stem — the same gesture as M, one arch fewer), lift, then start the *o* fresh at the baseline.

Practically: N-o is a break, not a join. Every month name starts this way, so this one habit fixes January through December at once.

## Inside the word: one crowd and one smooth run

After the break, "november" is almost entirely a smooth lowercase run — with one spot that betrays you:

- **o → v → e** — the *v* exits at x-height with a little up-flick that lands the *e* right on the baseline. Keep the v shallow; a deep v makes the next join feel like a valley;
- **e → m** — easy; the *m* is two round arches, same as the capital N's body;
- **m → b** — **the crowd spot.** The *b* sends an ascender straight up right after the *m*'s second arch, so the joint gets tight. Slow down for exactly one stroke: finish the second arch fully at the baseline, then climb the *b*. If your *mb* looks knotted, this is why;
- **b → e → r** — the *b*'s exit loop is high, the *e* dips, and the *r* ends with its little shoulder flick. Nothing to fear — just don't let the final *r* get lazy; that flick is what makes the word look finished.

Write it five times slowly and you'll notice the whole word sits in one zone: only N climbs above, and nothing descends below the baseline. "November" is a no-descender word — which is why it looks so tidy on a calendar header.

## Every month, ranked by difficulty

Once November is comfortable, the other months are free drills. Grouped by what they teach:

| Difficulty | Months | What it drills |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Warm-up | **May, June, July** | Short words; *J* is one of the rare capitals that dives below the line; *y* and its loop |
| Steady | **March, April, November** | The *pr* and *mb* junctions; capitals that don't connect |
| Watch out | **February** | The longest word — the *ru* join mid-word is where it falls apart |
| Descender work | **August, September** | Double *g* and double *p* descenders, all below the baseline |
| Boss level | **January, October, December** | The *Janu* climb; *O* is a full closed oval; *Dec* stacks a dive-and-return *D* after a break |

One honest note from coaching adults: **don't chase all twelve in one sitting.** One month per week as a header drill is plenty — by the time you've done four, the junction logic transfers and the rest are variations.

## People also ask

- **How do you write November in cursive?** Capital N (two arches, no join into the next letter), then *o-v-e-m-b-e-r* as a connected lowercase run — slow down only at the *m-b* junction.
- **Does the N connect to the o?** No. In cursive, capitals don't join to following lowercase letters; the same rule covers all twelve months.
- **Which months are hardest in cursive?** February (longest, awkward *ru* join) and the descender months August and September. May and June are the easiest warm-ups.
