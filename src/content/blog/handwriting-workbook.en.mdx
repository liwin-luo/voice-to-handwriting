# A handwriting workbook built from words you actually write

I printed a full alphabet workbook once and stopped at C. The rest of the pages were letters I already knew how to make. A handwriting workbook earns the second week when every line is a word you will write again before Friday.

Build one packet, use it for fifteen minutes a day for two weeks, then print the next list. Daily practice of that kind gets most adults to writing other people can read in four to six weeks. A new style takes longer than this packet, and this packet is only the pages.

## Mark one problem, then stop

Write this once, at the speed you sign a form: "Thanks — see you Monday."

Look at it and mark the loudest problem. The book drills that one thing.

- **Size jumps.** Tall letters and tiny letters share a word. The list wants short words with mixed heights: *tall, lily, July*.
- **The line wanders.** The last letter sits above or below the first. Pick words you can finish while watching the baseline: *date, paid, reply*.
- **Words crash together.** You cannot see the gap. Practice two-word lines: *thank you*, *next week*.
- **Slant changes inside a word.** Choose print or cursive for the whole book and leave it there.

One problem per book. A page that tries to fix size, spacing, and slant at once is the page that gets recycled.

## Fifteen lines, three jobs

Open the [handwriting workbook generator](/handwriting-workbook-generator). One item per line. Stop at fifteen. The box allows sixty, and filling it is the habit that wastes the paper: you copy a pile, and the hand learns the pile.

**Lines 1–5: the letters that went wrong** in that sentence. Lowercase, one per line. Five, chosen from the page you just wrote. Skip the rest of the alphabet.

**Lines 6–10: words you wrote this week.** Your first name, then four words from real notes. A vocabulary list from a website is someone else's week.

**Lines 11–15: short phrases you will write again.** Keep each line near thirty characters, about six short words. The generator draws the line once, at one size, and does not wrap. A full street address runs off the right edge.

```
s
a
y
d
k
your first name
Monday
thanks
invoice
reply
thank you
see you
next week
paid
best
```

Replace the five letters with the ones you circled, and replace "your first name" with your name. Keep the shape: five letters, five words, five short phrases.

## The settings that match an adult hand

| Control | Set it to | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Book title | A name you will recognize in a stack, such as "October drill" | The cover is the page with a blank line under "This book belongs to" |
| Word list | The fifteen lines above | Five items print on a page: three practice pages, plus the cover |
| Font | **Patrick Hand** for print. **Cedarville Cursive** if this book is a cursive rebuild | Dancing Script adds swashes. Useful on a card, noisy on a drill |
| Cover page | On | Sign the cover once. That signature is the sample you compare on day 14 |
| Example row | On | The first band is solid dark ink. The next two are dashed outlines of the same word |
| Page size | US Letter, or A4 if that is what your printer holds | The guides stay a top line, a dashed midline, and a baseline |

There is no row-height slider and no numbered stroke arrow. You are copying a shape on fixed bands. After the two dashed rows, write the line once more with no model on a sheet of [lined paper](/printable-paper). The workbook page itself has no empty copy line when every slot is filled.

## Fifteen minutes, the same PDF, fourteen days

**Minutes 1–3.** Open today's line. Name the problem you marked. Look at the dark row.

**Minutes 4–11.** Trace both dashed rows. Slow down about a quarter from the speed you used on the form. If the letter gets worse when you slow down, ease the grip and use a gel pen around 0.5–0.7 mm.

**Minutes 12–15.** Copy that line once, no model, on the lined sheet. Then stop.

| Days | The line | Done when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1–5 | One letter from the first five | Both dashed rows traced, one freehand copy |
| 6–7 | Whichever letter still breaks the size or the baseline | Same three passes. Leave the word pages closed |
| 8–12 | One word from the second five | The gap between letters stays even |
| 13 | One short phrase | The phrase still fits the line without shrinking |
| 14 | "Thanks — see you Monday," no model | Set it beside the signature on the cover |

Unused phrases wait for the next packet. Finishing every line in one Sunday sitting rehearses a tired hand. The night between sessions is part of the drill.

On day 14, look for the one problem you marked. It should show up less often. A different style will not be there yet. Print the next fifteen lines from whatever still looks wrong, and keep the same clock. The longer version of this, with the weekly levers spelled out, is the [four-week adult plan](/blog/how-to-improve-handwriting-adults).

## What this packet leaves out

A sentence that needs to wrap belongs on the [handwriting tool](/), one sentence you can actually read back. This generator is a word list.

A child's page needs a taller band and a shorter list. That is a different sheet: the [name tracing generator](/name-tracing) for a name, and [sight word tracing worksheets](/blog/sight-word-tracing-worksheets) when the folder comes home with eight words. The fonts here are open-source faces, the guides are an approximation of three-line paper, and nothing you type is uploaded.

Pain that stays after you slow down is the grip or the pen. Stop for the day. A wider barrel and less pressure come before another page. If it still hurts the next morning, have someone look at the hand.

## People also ask

- **What is a handwriting workbook?** A cover plus a short list, each line printed once in dark ink and twice as a dashed copy on three-line guides. Fifteen minutes a day. Then a new list.
- **What should an adult put in a handwriting workbook?** Five letters that went wrong in a sentence you just wrote, five words from this week's notes, and five short phrases you will write again.
- **How long does one handwriting workbook last?** Fourteen days. Then reprint from the letters that are still off. A finished book on the shelf is a book you are done practicing.
- **Should the workbook be print or cursive?** Print with Patrick Hand when your notes are print. Cedarville Cursive when you are rebuilding a signature. Keep one font for all fourteen days.
