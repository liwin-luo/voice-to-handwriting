# How to write in cursive: a beginner's guide that works

Most cursive tutorials hand you an alphabet chart and wish you luck. That skips the only two things that actually make cursive click: **consistent slant**, and **letters that share strokes**. Learn the strokes first and the 26 letters become six or seven small variations — not 26 separate problems.

This is the sequence handwriting teachers use, adapted for adult self-teachers. Type any word into the [cursive writing generator](/cursive) as you go, print it, and trace — the whole setup costs nothing.

## Start with the four strokes, not the letters

Every lowercase cursive letter is built from four moves:

1. **Undercurve** — the "uphill ramp" you enter most letters with, like a shallow *u* shape;
2. **Downstroke** — the main vertical pull, done in one smooth motion (this is where your slant lives);
3. **Overcurve** — the "hill" arch you exit with, like a shallow *n* hump;
4. **Connector** — the little tail at the baseline that hands off to the next letter.

Fill a few lines with each stroke alone — ramps, pulls, hills — before writing a single letter. If your downstrokes all lean the same way after ten minutes, you've done the hard part.

## Learn letters by family, not by alphabet order

| Family | Letters | The shared move |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ovals | a, c, d, g, o, q | Start with an overcurve into a round "c" shape |
| Waves | i, t, u, w, e | Up-and-down sawtooth strokes |
| Bumps | n, m, h, b, r, p | An arch (or two) planted on the baseline |
| Loops | l, f, k, j | A tall (or diving) loop on the downstroke |
| Odd ones | s, v, x, y, z | Individual shapes — save for last |

Practice families back-to-back: *c, a, d, g, o, q* in one sitting teaches you that they're all the same circle with different exits. Going a→b→c alphabet order teaches you 26 unrelated shapes.

## The connection rules (this is what makes it "cursive")

- Lowercase letters connect **at the baseline** through their exit tails;
- Four letters finish high and connect awkwardly: **o, b, v, w**. Let them stand alone or start the next letter fresh — fighting their connectors is the #1 beginner frustration;
- Keep **one consistent slant** (anything from 0° to ~5° is fine). A mixed slant reads as messy even when every letter is correct;
- **Spacing between words** should fit a pencil tip — connected handwriting gets crowded fast.

## The 30-day beginner plan

- **Week 1**: the four strokes + the oval family. Five minutes of warm-up strokes, then two letters a day;
- **Week 2**: waves + bumps families. Start writing real small words: *it, in, at, an, on*;
- **Week 3**: loops + odd ones, then all 26 lowercase in random order. Write your name and your city every day;
- **Week 4**: capitals (they don't connect as strictly — treat them as fancy cousins of print letters), then full sentences.

Trace → copy → compare beats pages of mindless loops. ## People also ask

- **What is the easiest way to learn cursive?** Strokes first, then letter families (not alphabet order), then connections — with daily 10–15 minute practice rather than weekly marathons.
- **How long does it take to learn cursive?** Most adults write readable cursive within 3–4 weeks of short daily practice; fluid, automatic cursive takes a couple of months.
- **Should I learn lowercase or uppercase cursive first?** Lowercase — it's 95% of what you write. Capitals are less standardized and can wait a couple of weeks.
- **Do all cursive letters connect?** No — *o, b, v* and *w* end high, and most handwriting styles let the next letter start fresh after them.
