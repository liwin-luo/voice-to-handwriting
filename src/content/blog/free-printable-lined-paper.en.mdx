# Free printable lined paper: college ruled, wide ruled & handwriting

You run out of lined paper at 9pm the night before school starts. Or you need **one page of graph paper**, and stores sell it in 100-sheet packs. Or your kid's teacher asks for "three-line paper" and you have no idea what that is.

All solvable with a printer: here's how to generate **free printable lined paper** — college ruled, wide ruled, graph, and three-line handwriting paper — in exactly the size and color you need, as a crisp PDF.

## Pick your paper type

| Type | Spacing | Who uses it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **College ruled** | Narrow (~7mm) | Middle school and up; fits more lines per page |
| **Wide ruled** | Wider (~8.7mm) | Elementary school; bigger targets for bigger handwriting |
| **Graph / grid** | Adjustable squares | Math, science, bullet-journal-style layouts |
| **Handwriting (3-line)** | Tall bands with dashed midline | Preschool–1st grade letter practice |
| **Blank** | — | Drawing, diagrams, covers |

Open the [printable paper generator](/printable-paper), tap a type, and the preview redraws instantly.

College-ruled US Letter holds about 220 words on one side at a normal handwriting size. Wide ruled holds fewer, around 180. If the assignment is already written, the [handwriting page calculator](/handwriting-page-calculator) counts the sheets for that paper before you print the blank page.

## Tune it like a print shop would

Three settings do most of the work:

- **Line spacing** — the default sits near college-ruled spacing; slide it wider for young writers. For graph paper, the same slider sets the square size;
- **Line color** — classic pale blue prints *much* lighter than you'd expect. If the lines overpower the writing (common with dyslexic or low-vision students), switch to light gray;
- **Red margin** — the vertical line on the left, like a legal pad. Toggle it off for full-width pages or hole-punched binders.

Then choose **Letter (8.5×11")** or **A4** — Letter is the US default — and download the PDF. The page is drawn at printer resolution, so lines come out crisp, not blurry-scan crisp.

## The handwriting paper most parents are actually looking for

"Three-line paper" (top line, dashed middle line, baseline) is the standard for teaching letter formation — the dashed line shows where lowercase letters stop, and the top line catches tall letters like *b* and *k*.

If you're not sure how to explain it to your child, read [kindergarten handwriting paper, explained](/blog/kindergarten-handwriting-paper) — and if you want those same three lines with your child's **name printed on them to trace**, use the [name tracing generator](/name-tracing) instead; it's the same band system with the words filled in.

- Spacing values are close approximations of standard ruling, not manufacturing-exact — fine for notes and homework, don't use them to calibrate lab equipment;
- The margin line sits about ¾" from the left edge (narrower than a legal pad's); flip the toggle off if you need the full margin for punching;
- Each download is one page — print the PDF multiple times or use your printer's "copies" setting for stacks.
